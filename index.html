Brain Tumor Segmentation with U-Net

Rutgers, team of fourWork shared equally across the teamMachine learning course

  • PyTorch
  • U-Net
  • BraTS 2020
  • Medical imaging

Multi-class segmentation of tumor sub-regions (necrotic core, edema, enhancing tumor) from 369 BraTS 2020 patient volumes. All four MRI modalities (T1, T1CE, T2, FLAIR) are z-score normalized per modality and stacked as input channels over 57,000+ 2D slices. The model is trained with a combined Dice and cross-entropy loss, with early stopping on validation Dice.

What held up

Validation Dice stabilized around 0.88 against about 0.91 on training, a small gap. Predicted masks matched ground truth in shape and boundary on held-out slices.

Incentive-Compatible Rewards for Confidence Reporting

Rutgers, team of fourMechanism design and RL

  • Q-learning
  • Contextual bandits
  • Reward design
  • Calibration

A helper-bot agent chooses between a fast answer and an accurate one across easy, hard, and ambiguous queries, then reports its confidence. We compared four reward designs to see which ones produce agents that are both useful and honest about uncertainty.

What held up

A speed-only reward produced textbook specification gaming: the agent chose Fast almost every time, whatever the query. A balanced reward gave a sensible context-dependent policy. Penalizing confidence reports that didn't match the agent's own softmax-derived belief pushed honesty close to 100% and raised accuracy on ambiguous queries.

What I logged

The environment is static, with fixed accuracy probabilities. Real task distributions drift, and an agent would need to update both its beliefs and its reporting strategy as they do.

Structural Analysis of the Spotify Tracks Dataset

RutgersMarch to May 2026

  • PCA
  • t-SNE
  • UMAP
  • GMM
  • VAE

An unsupervised study of about 114,000 songs across 114 genres to find which of ten audio features carry musical signal and which are noise, followed by a 3D variational autoencoder in PyTorch to generate synthetic tracks.

What held up

PCA, t-SNE, and UMAP independently pointed to the same dominant “intensity” axis, built from energy, loudness, and acousticness. Generated output was checked with KS statistics and cluster analysis.

What I logged

Rent vs. Own: Cloud Cost Decision Framework

Rutgers, team of fiveMarch to April 2026

  • Cost modeling
  • Sensitivity analysis
  • AWS pricing

A five-year model comparing AWS reserved and on-demand compute against owned co-located hardware for Shopify (about 20 baseline instances) and Netflix's origin tier (about 2,400), under stable, spiky, and high-growth workloads. I led the cost-model architecture and the Shopify case study, and designed the sensitivity analysis.

What held up

Workload shape, not company size, decided the answer. The break-even hardware cost came out near $12.2K per server in both cases despite a 100× difference in scale, because it depends on per-instance economics. Scenario winners survived ±20% cloud pricing and +30% hardware cost.

What I logged

Staffing costs are excluded from the base model, and the conclusions assume refurbished hardware. At new-hardware prices of $15K or more per server, owning stops beating reserved cloud.

Autonomous Agents Under Uncertainty

Rutgers CS520, three projectsFebruary to May 2025

  • A* variants
  • Bayesian inference
  • CNN
  • Simulation

A series of spaceship-grid problems about acting well on incomplete information.

  • Fire spread. Four path-planning bots navigating a spreading fire. A risk-aware bot that adds fire-spread probability to Adaptive A* path costs outperformed plain and repeated A*, especially at high flammability, over 200 trials per setting.
  • Space rat tracking. Bayesian belief updates over every open cell from a noisy distance sensor, plus a diffusion step once the rat moves. My redesigned search bot won about 95% of runs with low sensor sensitivity. The simpler baseline overtook it as sensitivity rose, and I kept that result in the report.
  • Localization. A CNN trained on 47,570 simulated belief states from 200 mazes predicts how many moves localization will take. That estimate drives one-step lookahead policies, which gave modest improvements over the rule-based baseline on larger belief sets.

The Erosion of Cloud Trust: Why Large-Scale Users Will Return to Private Infrastructure

Position paper, March 2026

The paper argues that three pressures on trust in the public cloud will push large organizations toward private, hybrid, and multi-cloud setups: rising security incidents driven by misconfiguration, lock-in built into pricing and egress fees, and outages whose blast radius spans thousands of tenants. It takes the strongest case for public cloud seriously before answering it, drawing on repatriation cases such as 37signals, Dropbox, and GEICO, and on the October 2025 AWS US-EAST-1 outage.
